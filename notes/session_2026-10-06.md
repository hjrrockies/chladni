## What was decided about `douse` (recorded in `docs/design/`)

`docs/design/douse_requirements.md` -- from an interview with the user and a read of `douse`'s
solver-facing modules:

* **Needs**: polygons up to N = 16 (convex, several reentrant corners, near-regular and exactly
  symmetric; short edges, near-collinear vertices, thin shapes); `lam_k` to k ~ 10-30; `prec`
  chosen per solve, tightened as the optimizer converges, working default 1e-13 today; certified
  bounds; Hadamard integrals; 10 s - 1 min per solve, i.e. 1-7 hours per optimization start
  (confirmed acceptable); on a shortfall, the best result plus an honest bound and flags.
* **The line**: `douse` owns parameterizations, the chain rule, cluster grouping, the `prec`
  schedule and re-plan decisions. **`chladni` assumes no parameterization** -- in particular no
  vertex velocities (user's direction). `douse` may be changed for a better workflow; it is not
  held to `lappy`'s API.
* **The interface**: a sorted `Spectrum`, every level certified, the count part of the contract, a
  level straddling `n` returned whole; `warm=` seeding carries the cold-solve contract; a frozen,
  recordable `Plan` realized per iterate, **re-planned explicitly when `prec` changes** (user's
  decision) and routinely on drift; `spec.hadamard(levels, V)` with `V(curve_index, t)` a per-curve
  callable normal velocity (user's decision), batched, the boundary-condition formula applied by
  `chladni`, built in each member's own eigenfunctions.
* **Why each item**: the doc ties it to a `lappy` workaround in `douse` (sorting after tracking,
  `_verify_multiplicity`, `track_ttol`, exception wrapping, `detect_cluster`, the Lambda/M basis
  mismatch, `spurious_tol`, the Weyl guard, `replan_tol`).

`docs/design/douse_failure_cases.md` -- every recorded `lappy` failure in `douse` and `lappy`,
de-duplicated, tiered by reproducibility and ground truth. The headline: **the most damaging cases
were silent** -- a triangle missing three modes at tension 2.8e-14, a plan that invents a double
`lam_1` with a 42% objective error, a bit-identical triple and a double `lam_1` that passed
`verified=True`. A benchmark must score against reference spectra WITH multiplicities, and most
Tier 2 shapes need references generated (high-`prec` solve cross-checked against FEM).

## The L-shape tour (`examples/lshape_tour.ipynb`, `c44bd60`)

Built in the order of the user's own `lappy` notebooks (`lshape_convergence_test`,
`mps_diagnostics`, `mps demo`), with a table mapping each `lappy` call to its `chladni` counterpart:
domain, basis, collocation, normalization, solver, tension curve, a hand minimization, convergence
in `n`, the certified search, `Spectrum`, eigenfunctions. Every claim asserted; ~20 s. Plotting
cells end in `plt.show()` (the user asked about the convention: it suppresses stray reprs and keeps
cells script-portable). What it measured:

* **scipy's bounded Brent cannot refine an eigenvalue past ~1.5e-8 relative**: it adds
  `sqrt(eps)*|x|` to `xatol` internally (`scipy/optimize/_optimize.py`, `tol1`). Golden section
  reached 7.6e-15. A concrete reason the refine stage is `chladni`'s own.
* **On the L-shape at `prec = 1e-8`, Moler-Payne and `delta_res` are comparable** (MP tighter on 4 of
  7), not the 2-3 orders apart measured on rectangles in `notes/moler_payne_vs_tension.md`.
* **`FourierBessel`'s per-entry `PrecisionWarning` does not track eigenvalue accuracy**: with the
  family at its default `prec` it fires on some runs only; at `prec=1e-12` it fires 185 times in
  one search; the eigenvalues agree to ~4e-14 either way. Left in the notebook as an open question
  (`helmholtz/_core.py`, `_check_accuracy`).
* Convergence: ~0.21 decades per corner term, flat near 1e-14 by ~70 terms; plane waves get
  `lam_1` to one digit but `2 pi^2` to 2e-15 (that eigenfunction has no `r^(2/3)` term).

Corrections the user made, now in the notebook: every Dirichlet eigenfunction VANISHES at the
reentrant corner -- generically like `r^(2/3)`, which is why the gradient blows up; the first draft
said the opposite.

## What reading the code turned up

* **`is_reflection_closed` is not `is_singular`, and the notebook first confused them.**
  `Corner.is_reflection_closed` is `omega = pi/m`, an angle fact; "singular" needs the BC offset and
  belongs to `CornerModes.is_singular` (`docs/conventions.md`). They part at BC junctions: a
  straight Dirichlet/Neumann junction is reflection-closed but singular; a right angle with a N/D
  junction is neither singular nor reflection-open. The tour now asks `CornerModes`. The name stays.
  `tests/hard_roster.py:nonanalytic()` uses the same shortcut, harmless on its single-BC roster.
* **`break_ts` / `break_taus` returning `np.empty(0)` is intended**: the base-class default for
  smooth segments; `ParametricSegment` and `SplineSegment` override (knots), and `quadrature.py`
  breaks panels there.
* **A BC junction is a (non-geometric) corner** -- the user's settled wording, no "vertex" (it
  collides with polygon vertices). `geometry.md`'s stale `Corner.is_singular` sketch line,
  `conventions.md`, the `Corner`/`Boundary.corners` docstrings and the geometry tour now say it
  (`03c29db`).
* **The "2.83x radius" in `at_corner`'s docstring is ambiguous, not wrong.** It is the WORST corner
  ratio of `evaluation_radius` to `clearance` on the L-shape (the far convex corners), from a
  measurement with families at every corner; at the reentrant corner it is 1.41x. Claude first
  called it wrong. A doc-only clarification is optional.
* **The radius scaling does not affect accuracy**: clearance-scaled columns up to 3e26 gave the
  same eigenvalues and bounds, because equilibration undoes any fixed column scale. It guards
  overflow at high orders and raw-column users.
* **`Spectrum.bound` under-reports on a starved basis** -- the session's most important finding.
  14 FB terms at each of six corners, `prec = 1e-12`: on 4 of 7 levels `bound = min(delta_res,
  mp_bound)` is below the true error (29.52167 off by 1.9e-4 against `delta_res = 4.4e-11`), and
  `attained_prec` is wrong with it. Moler-Payne covers every level; `delta_res` is not an upper
  bound there. `TODO.md` section 4, repro `notes/delta_res_undercover.py` (`03f13d6`). One case;
  adequate bases and the penalty path untested.
* **`FourierBessel`'s `PrecisionWarning` is genuine but not substantive.** The series branch's
  `rel_err = eps * largest_term / |S|` blows up at entries near a zero of `J_mu(k r)`. On the
  L-shape the worst bound, 2.1e-8 (true 2.7e-9 by mpmath), sits on an entry 1.9e-6 of its column
  maximum: 5e-15 relative to the column, at most 2.6e-13 over all entries. The solve is normwise
  (equilibration, GSVD), so eigenvalues are ~4e-14 whether it fires once or 185 times. **The user's
  rule: warn only for precision losses that are substantive** -- that can move a computed result.
  `TODO.md` section 4, repro `notes/precision_warning_entrywise.py` (`42c2a77`).
* **The factored Bessel kernel's end-to-end advantage over plain scipy is unproven.** It is
  validated as a kernel against mpmath and is faster than `jv` + `jvp` when derivatives are wanted.
  But raw `jv` columns, underflowed ones dropped, give identical eigenvalues on the L-shape
  (1.6e-14) and indistinguishable ones on `douse`'s spike triangle at 1e-9 .. 2e-8, even with 16-20
  apex columns lost to underflow -- the same normwise reason. A simplification candidate, decided
  by two untested cases: a high-precision basis on a sharp shape, and derivatives (Neumann, Cauchy
  data). `TODO.md` section 4 `[later]`, repro `notes/bessel_kernel_vs_scipy.py` (`de3df42`). A
  first run used 8-digit vertices and was confounded at 1e-8; discarded.

The thread through the last three: **after equilibration only column-relative error matters**, so
per-entry accuracy machinery (the warning, much of the kernel) has to earn its place end to end.
