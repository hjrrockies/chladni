\noindent These notes derive the regularizations measured in \code{notes/smooth\_filter.md}
and implemented as \code{reg="tikhonov"} and \code{reg="tikhonov\_qr"} in
\code{chladni.mps}, plus the second-order variant under test. Statements are marked as
\emph{proved} or \emph{measured}; the measured ones cite the note's sections.

\section{Setting}

At a parameter $\lam$ the method of particular solutions assembles a boundary block
$\AB \in \R^{m_B\times n}$ and an interior block $\AI \in \R^{m_I\times n}$, one column per basis
function, and equilibrates so that every column of the stack
\[
  A = \begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \AI\end{bmatrix} \in \R^{m\times n}, \qquad m = m_B + m_I,
\]
has unit norm. The \emph{tension} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tension}
  \sig_1(\lam) = \min_{c\neq 0} \frac{\norm{\AB c}}{\norm{\AI c}},
\end{equation}
the smallest generalized singular value of the pair $(\AB,\AI)$. Write the SVD of the stack as
$A = U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$ with $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_n\ge 0)$, and split
$U$ by rows as $U = \begin{bmatrix}U_B\\ U_I\end{bmatrix}$. After equilibration
$\norm{A}_F = \sqrt{n}$ (a column that underflowed to exact zeros keeps norm $0$).

The basis is over-complete, so $\Sigma$ is a smooth ramp that meets a flat floor of rounding
noise at $s_j/s_1 \approx 10^{-16}$. Directions near that floor carry no signal; the whole
question is how to keep them out of \eqref{eq:tension}.

\section{The invariance that rules out a weighting}

\begin{lemma}[proved]\label{lem:invariance}
For any invertible $M\in\R^{n\times n}$ the generalized singular values of $(\AB M,\AI M)$ equal
those of $(\AB,\AI)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The substitution $c = Mc'$ is a bijection of $\R^n\setminus\{0\}$, and
\[
  \frac{\norm{\AB M c'}}{\norm{\AI M c'}} = \frac{\norm{\AB c}}{\norm{\AI c}}.
\]
The min--max characterization of every generalized singular value is therefore unchanged.
\end{proof}

A column weighting $w_j = f(s_j/s_1)$ is a right factor $M=\mathrm{diag}(w)$, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} it changes nothing until some $w_j = 0$ exactly (measured: $4\times
10^{-15}$ change under a random invertible $M$). Truncation works \emph{because} its projector is
singular. A continuous regularization must therefore change the \emph{objective}, not the
coordinates: it must be a penalty.

\section{Truncation, in whitened coordinates}

Let $y = \Sigma V^{\mathsf T} c$, so that $Ac = Uy$ and \eqref{eq:tension} becomes
$\min_y \norm{U_B y}/\norm{U_I y}$. Rank truncation at $r$ restricts $y$ to its first $r$
coordinates: the pencil $(U_{B,1},U_{I,1})$ with $U_1 = U[:,1{:}r]$, and back map
$c = V_1\Sigma_1^{-1}y$. The threshold rule re-derives $r(\lam) = \#\{j: s_j \ge
\mathrm{rtol}\cdot s_1\}$ at every $\lam$, and $r$ is a step function: wherever a noisy $s_j$ sits
near the cutoff, rounding decides it, and the rank flickers. Measured: 63--88 changes per
201 samples at every half-width from $2\times10^{-2}$ down to $2\times10^{-10}$ (\S3 of the
note).

\section{Order-1 Tikhonov}

\begin{definition}
For $\tau>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tik1}
  \sig_\tau(\lam) = \min_{c\ne 0}
  \frac{\sqrt{\norm{\AB c}^2 + \tau^2\norm{c}^2}}{\norm{\AI c}},
\end{equation}
which is the smallest generalized singular value of $\left(\begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \tau I\end{bmatrix},
\AI\right)$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Whitened form.} With $y=\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}c$ (assume $s_j>0$; otherwise take limits),
$\tau^2\norm{c}^2 = \tau^2\norm{\Sigma^{-1}y}^2 = \sum_j g_j^2 y_j^2$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g1}
  g_j = \frac{\tau}{s_j},
\end{equation}
so \eqref{eq:tik1} is the pencil
$\left(\begin{bmatrix}U_B\\ \mathrm{diag}(g)\end{bmatrix}, U_I\right)$. A weight $g_j=0$ leaves a
direction alone; $g_j\to\infty$ forbids it, which is truncation. The corresponding filter factor is
\[
  f_j = \frac{1}{1+g_j^2} = \frac{s_j^2}{s_j^2+\tau^2},
\]
a one-decade-wide smooth step centred at $s_j=\tau$. The augmented numerator has full column rank
for every $\tau>0$, and \eqref{eq:tik1} is continuous in $(\AB,\AI)$: no rank is chosen, so none
can change.

\paragraph{Scaling.} Scaling the whole pencil by $\alpha$ divides $\tau$'s effect by $\alpha$, so a
scale-invariant choice is relative:
\[
  \tau = \mathrm{rtol}\cdot\norm{A}_F = \mathrm{rtol}\cdot\sqrt{n}
  \quad\text{(equilibrated)},
\]
a constant for the pencil's life. The penalty acts in equilibrated coordinates, which is what makes
$\norm{c}$ meaningful: raw Fourier--Bessel columns span about $1149$ nats of scale.

\section{Two realizations: identical on paper, not in LAPACK}

\paragraph{Literal (\code{"tikhonov"}).} Hand the GSVD the pair $\left(\begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \tau
I\end{bmatrix},\AI\right)$ directly. Back map: the identity.

\paragraph{QR (\code{"tikhonov\_qr"}).} Take an unpivoted QR
\[
  M_\tau = \begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \tau I\\ \AI\end{bmatrix} = QR,
  \qquad Q = \begin{bmatrix}Q_1\\ Q_2\end{bmatrix},
\]
with $Q_1$ the rows belonging to $\begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \tau I\end{bmatrix}$, and hand the GSVD
$(Q_1,Q_2)$. Back map: $c = R^{-1}y$.

\begin{proposition}[proved]\label{prop:same}
The two realizations have identical generalized singular values in exact arithmetic.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$M_\tau$ has full column rank because of the $\tau I$ block, so $R$ is invertible and
$(Q_1,Q_2) = \left(\begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \tau I\end{bmatrix}R^{-1},\ \AI R^{-1}\right)$. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} with $M = R^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why they differ in floating point (measured).} $\tau I$ makes the \emph{stack} full rank
but does nothing for the \emph{interior} block. LAPACK's \code{?ggsvp3} decides the numerical
rank of that block with its own tolerance, about $\max(m,n)\norm{A}\eps$. So the literal form
moves the rank decision inside LAPACK rather than removing it: its count of finite tensions ran
$41$--$50$ over a scan where the threshold took seven values. The QR form gives LAPACK an
orthonormal stack, $Q_1^{\mathsf T}Q_1 + Q_2^{\mathsf T}Q_2 = I$, and the problem is then a CS
decomposition with nothing to decide: the count held at $n$ (or $m_I$) everywhere measured. The
literal form's LAPACK cut is also why its floor stalls near $9\times10^{-14}$; the QR form's does
not (\S10 of the note).

\section{Order 1: the floor, and why it cannot be traded against the noise}

\begin{proposition}[proved]\label{prop:floor1}
Let $c^\ast$ satisfy $\norm{\AB c^\ast} = \epsilon\,\norm{\AI c^\ast}$ (an eigenfunction resolved to
$\epsilon$ by the basis), and let $\rho = \norm{c^\ast}/\norm{\AI c^\ast}$. Then
\[
  \sig_\tau \le \sqrt{\epsilon^2 + \tau^2\rho^2}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Evaluate the quotient in \eqref{eq:tik1} at $c^\ast$.
\end{proof}

Measured, the floor is $1.38$--$1.45\,\tau$ from $\tau = 1.7\times10^{-11}$ down to
$1.7\times10^{-14}$, so $\rho\approx1.45$; it rises to $1.9\,\tau$ at $\tau=1.1\times10^{-15}$, where
the basis's own $\epsilon$ enters, exactly as the bound says. That bottom value, $2.1\times10^{-15}$,
is within $1.3\times$ of the threshold's $1.6\times10^{-15}$.
The eigenfunction lives in well-conditioned directions, yet it still pays $\tau\norm{c^\ast}$: the
order-1 penalty charges every direction, the signal included.

\paragraph{Noise (heuristic, and measured).} Assembly and the QR are backward stable, so each is
exact for a perturbation $\delta A$ with $\norm{\delta A}\approx\eps\norm{A}$. In a noise direction
the only thing holding the minimizer off it is the penalty, of size $\tau$, against rounding of
size $\eps\norm{A}$. As $\tau$ falls toward $\eps\norm{A}\approx 10^{-15}$, a weak penalty lets the
minimizer lean on directions whose content is rounding. Measured: relative noise in $\sig_\tau$ is
$1\times10^{-2}$ at the $\tau$ a $5\times10^{-15}$ floor needs, against the threshold's
$1\times10^{-3}$, and it is \emph{multiplicative}, uniform along a well's flank. Floor and noise are
both set by $\tau$ against $\eps\norm{A}$, so no single $\tau$ makes both small.

\section{Order 2: a penalty that spares the signal}

The fix is to make the penalty depend on the direction: tiny on well-conditioned directions,
large on noise. In whitened coordinates this is $g_j = (\tau_2/s_j)^2$ in place of
\eqref{eq:g1}. The point is that it can be built from triangular factors, without an SVD.

\begin{lemma}[proved]\label{lem:rinvt}
If $A = Q_0R_0$ is an economic QR with $R_0$ invertible, then
$\norm{R_0^{-\mathsf T}c} = \norm{\Sigma^{-1}V^{\mathsf T}c}$ for all $c$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$R_0^{\mathsf T}R_0 = A^{\mathsf T}A = V\Sigma^2V^{\mathsf T}$, so $R_0 = W\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$ for
some orthogonal $W$ (its singular values are $\Sigma$ and its right singular vectors $V$). Hence
$R_0^{-\mathsf T} = W\Sigma^{-1}V^{\mathsf T}$, and $W$ preserves norms.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The bounded form.} $R_0$ is singular when a column underflows, and near-singular
always. Use instead the factor $R_1$ of the $\tau_1$-augmented stack, which \code{"tikhonov\_qr"}
already computes:
\[
  \begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \tau_1 I\\ \AI\end{bmatrix} = Q_1'R_1,\qquad
  R_1^{\mathsf T}R_1 = A^{\mathsf T}A + \tau_1^2 I = V(\Sigma^2 + \tau_1^2 I)V^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By the argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:rinvt},
$\norm{R_1^{-\mathsf T}c} = \norm{(\Sigma^2+\tau_1^2I)^{-1/2}V^{\mathsf T}c}$, so
$\norm{R_1^{-\mathsf T}}\le 1/\tau_1$: bounded, and invertible whatever $A$ does.

\begin{definition}
The order-2 penalized tension is the smallest generalized singular value of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tik2}
  \left(\begin{bmatrix}\AB\\ \tau_1 I\\ \tau_2^2 R_1^{-\mathsf T}\end{bmatrix},\ \AI\right),
\end{equation}
realized, like \code{"tikhonov\_qr"}, by an unpivoted QR of the numerator stacked on $\AI$ and a
GSVD of the two row blocks of its $Q$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Whitened form.} With $y = \Sigma V^{\mathsf T}c$,
$\tau_2^4\norm{R_1^{-\mathsf T}c}^2 = \sum_j \big(\tau_2^2/(s_j\sqrt{s_j^2+\tau_1^2})\big)^2 y_j^2$.
Together with the $\tau_1 I$ rows, direction $j$ carries the weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g2}
  g_j^2 = \Big(\frac{\tau_1}{s_j}\Big)^2
        + \Big(\frac{\tau_2^2}{s_j\sqrt{s_j^2+\tau_1^2}}\Big)^2
  \;\approx\;
  \begin{cases}
    (\tau_2/s_j)^4 & \tau_1 \ll s_j \quad\text{(order 2)},\\[2pt]
    (\tau_2^2/(\tau_1 s_j))^2 & s_j \ll \tau_1 \quad\text{(order 1, bounded)},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
taking $\tau_1\ll\tau_2$ so that the first term is negligible wherever it matters. In basis
coordinates the block $\tau_2^2R_1^{-\mathsf T}$ is dense lower-triangular, not diagonal. It is
diagonal only in the SVD basis, and Lemma~\ref{lem:rinvt} is what lets us apply it without forming
that basis.

\paragraph{Floor.} Proposition~\ref{prop:floor1} carries over with the penalty term replaced:
\[
  \sig \le \sqrt{\epsilon^2 + \tau_1^2\rho^2
   + \tau_2^4\,\norm{(\Sigma^2+\tau_1^2)^{-1/2}V^{\mathsf T}c^\ast}^2/\norm{\AI c^\ast}^2 }.
\]
If $c^\ast$ lies in directions with $s_j\ge s_\ast\gg\tau_2$, the last term under the root is at
most $(\tau_2^2\rho/s_\ast)^2$, and $\tau_2^2\rho/s_\ast\ll\tau_2\rho$. The signal is charged by $(\tau_2/s_j)^2$, not
$\tau_2/s_j$, so the floor can sit far \emph{below} $\tau_2$.

\paragraph{Noise (heuristic).} In a noise direction ($s_j\approx\eps\norm{A}$) the weight is
$(\tau_2/s_j)^2$ rather than $\tau/s_j$: the margin over rounding is the \emph{square} of the
order-1 margin at the same $\tau$.

\paragraph{Measured} (\S10 of the note; family A, $n=126$, $\tau_1 = 10^{-16}\sqrt n$):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule

\midrule

At a matched $\approx5\times10^{-15}$ floor, order 2 is about $270\times$ quieter than order 1 and
$27\times$ quieter than the threshold. On the one $\pr=10^{-14}$ decline-grid cell checked so far
($145\pi^2$, band $1.3$), the threshold declines on ``the slope estimate never settled'' and order 2
brackets all ten levels, pure, in $280$ evaluations.

\section{Relation to the hybrid}

Section 6b of the note measured a ``hybrid'': an SVD of the stack, a hard cut below $\eps s_1$, and
$g_j = (c\,s_1/s_j)^p$. At $p=2$ that is \eqref{eq:g2} with an explicit SVD and a hard cut. Order 2
recovers the same filter shape with two QRs and one triangular inverse, and without either the SVD
or a cut that a singular value could cross.
