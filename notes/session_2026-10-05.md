## The reorientation (end of session) -- this sets the priorities

The user's framing: **`chladni` exists to fix the `lappy` failures that hurt `douse` -- dropped
eigenvalues and lost precision.** Much of this session, and of the weeks before it, chased 1e-12
and 1e-14 on a synthetic cluster grid instead. Points agreed:

* **`douse` still cannot call `chladni` on a domain it generates.** `MPSEigensolver` needs a
  hand-built basis; the basis planner has been `[blocking]` all along. No `Eigenproblem`, no
  Hadamard, no inner-loop timing.
* **`chladni` already delivers 1e-14 on ordinary eigenvalues** -- the threshold path on the disk and
  triangle matched to 2-6e-16, and M3 now certifies it. What failed "at 1e-14" was the cluster
  grid's 1e-14 rung, which asks for members ~3e-15 relative apart -- about 13 ulps. That is a
  stress test at the edge of float64, not the gate for practical precision.
* **The precision-aware design paid off as honesty, not as an easier regularizer**: `prec`-derived
  tolerances, declines and flags instead of silent drops, and a certificate per level.
* **The easier path to high precision is the planner, not the regularizer**: build a basis, solve,
  read the certified bound, enrich until it meets `prec` -- `CLAUDE.md`'s black-box loop.
* The side search is accreting single-cell special cases, all from one rectangle family. Each is
  defensible; together they are an overfitting and maintenance risk the planner may disturb.

## Open, in the order I would take them

1. **Find out what `douse` needs**: precision, eigenvalue indices, the domain classes it generates,
   eigenfunctions and derivatives, a per-solve time budget. Read `douse` first; nothing in this
   repository records its requirements.
2. **A `douse`-shaped acceptance benchmark**: domains `douse` actually generates, including the ones
   where `lappy` dropped eigenvalues or lost precision, through `lappy` and `chladni`, scored on
   dropped eigenvalues, certified precision (Moler-Payne) and wall time -- with a 1e-14 tier on
   representative domains. Expectation, unmeasured: the threshold path plus certification passes
   much of it, and what fails points at the basis.
3. **A minimal basis planner**, judged by the certified bound: corner Fourier-Bessel at singular
   corners plus a size rule. Then `Eigenproblem`, then M5 Hadamard, then speed.
4. **CI determinism**: `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` in `tests.yml` (0 failures in ~39 single-thread
   Linux runs, 2.5-3.2x faster). It hides the flip rather than removing it -- the user's call. Then
   decide when to push `integrals`.
5. **Parked until a `douse` case asks**: the posterior mode to extend `reg="auto"` to 1e-12; the 1e-14
   span problem; the second-order penalty.

## The lesson worth carrying

**Measure the claim before writing it, and measure it where it will be used.** This session's two
large reversals were both summaries that outran their evidence, and the larger drift -- weeks of
precision work on a synthetic grid while the black-box path stayed blocked -- is the same mistake
at the scale of a project. The `douse` benchmark is the instrument that keeps the next step honest.
